## What You've Covered

Across eight modules, you've worked through the bulk of CompTIA Linux+ (XK0-005) **Domain 1 (System Management)**, **Domain 2 (Security)**, and a good portion of **Domain 4 (Troubleshooting)** — filesystem navigation, permissions, users and groups, the full storage stack, boot and init, process/memory/kernel management, hardware and package management, networking, and system security.

---

## The Gap This Course Doesn't Close

As flagged back in Module 1: this course does **not** cover **Domain 3 (Scripting, Containers & Automation)**, which is roughly a sixth of the current exam. Before scheduling your test, make sure you can comfortably:

- Write a Bash script using variables, conditionals (`if`/`case`), loops (`for`/`while`), and functions
- Use basic Git commands (`clone`, `add`, `commit`, `push`, `pull`, branching)
- Explain the difference between a container and a VM, and run basic `docker`/`podman` commands (`run`, `build`, `ps`, `images`)
- Recognize the shape of a basic Ansible playbook and what "infrastructure as code" means in practice

CompTIA's own official **exam objectives PDF** (search "CompTIA Linux+ XK0-005 exam objectives" on comptia.org) is the authoritative source for exactly what's testable — cross-check it against this course before your exam date, since exam objectives do get revised.

## Exam Logistics

| Detail | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Format | A mix of multiple-choice and **performance-based questions** (PBQs) — simulated terminal tasks where you actually type commands, not just pick an answer |
| Testing provider | Pearson VUE — in-person at a testing center or online-proctored from home |
| Registration | Through CompTIA's own store or an authorized training partner; check with your academic department first for any available student pricing or vouchers before paying full price |

<Pitfall title="PBQs Reward Muscle Memory, Not Just Recognition">

Performance-based questions ask you to actually solve a small task in a simulated terminal — configure a permission, fix an `/etc/fstab` entry, chase down a process. Passive reading gets you through multiple-choice questions; PBQs are why building and breaking a real lab VM (Module 1) matters so much more than memorizing tables alone.

</Pitfall>

---

## How to Use the Time Before Your Exam

1. **Rebuild every command table in this course from memory, in your lab VM**, not on paper — if you can't type it, you don't know it yet.
2. **Break something on purpose every study session.** Corrupt a permission, remove a fstab entry, lock an account, then fix it under a self-imposed time limit.
3. **Alternate between your RHEL-family and Debian-family VMs** so command syntax differences (`dnf` vs `apt`, `firewalld` vs `ufw`) stop tripping you up.
4. **Take a full-length practice exam** under real time pressure before test day — CompTIA sells official practice exams, and knowing your weak domains ahead of time is far better than discovering them live.
