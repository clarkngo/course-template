## 🧩 What This Course Actually Taught

Look back at the five celebration moments, in order:

| Module | Celebration moment |
|---|---|
| 1. First Spark | You don't have to earn the win — you get to have it first |
| 2. The Loop | A rough draft isn't broken, only unfinished |
| 3. Under the Hood | Errors are clues, not verdicts |
| 4. Good Taste | You already had taste — you just needed to trust it |
| 5. Ship It | There's no final boss waiting — you've already been playing the whole game |

None of these were really about vibe coding specifically. They're about how to learn *anything* by doing it, staying curious when it breaks, and trusting your own judgment about whether the result is good. Vibe coding was just the fastest way to make that loop concrete and immediate.

---

## 🤔 What Vibe Coding Can't Replace

Being honest about limits is part of good judgment, not a discouragement:

- **It doesn't replace understanding your users.** No amount of AI-written code fixes an app that solves the wrong problem.
- **It doesn't replace caution with sensitive data.** The privacy judgment calls from Module 4 only get more important as your projects get bigger.
- **It doesn't remove the need for testing.** Every loop in this course depended on you actually running and checking your work — that never becomes optional, no matter how good the AI gets.

What it *does* replace is the old requirement that you personally learn a programming language before you're allowed to build something real. That barrier is gone, permanently, for the kinds of projects you've been making.

---

## 🚀 Where to Go From Here

There's no next mandatory step — just more reps, on bigger ideas, using the exact same loop:

- Rebuild your capstone with a feature you didn't have time for
- Show it to someone and watch where they get confused (a live version of the stranger test)
- Pick a new idea, bigger than "small enough to finish today," and let it take a few sittings
