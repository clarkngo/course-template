## 🛠️ Learning by Doing

No slides. No vocabulary list. Just open the tool you picked in Module 0 and build something.

<CodeLab title="Your First Build">

**Step 1 — Open your tool and describe your app.**

Copy this prompt, or write your own idea instead:

> "Build me a simple web app: a personal 'about me' page with my name, three fun facts about me, and a button that shows a random fun fact when clicked. Use a clean, friendly design with a soft blue color scheme."

**Step 2 — Run it.**

Most tools show you a working preview right in the chat, or give you a button to open it. Click it. Look at what actually appeared.

Don't see a preview anywhere? Don't hunt around guessing — ask directly: *"Where do I click to see this running?"* (Module 0 covers why this works every time, even as interfaces change.)

**Step 3 — Poke at it.**

Click the button a few times. Does it show a different fact? Does anything look off? Don't fix anything yet — just notice.

**Step 4 — Tell someone (even if it's just yourself).**

Say out loud what you just did: *"I described an app in plain English, and it exists now."* That sentence is the entire point of this lesson.

</CodeLab>

## 🔍 Notice What You Did *Not* Do

You didn't write a single line of code. You didn't open a technical manual. You didn't debug a syntax error before anything ran. You described an outcome, and a working version of that outcome appeared.

Hold onto exactly what that felt like — confusing, exciting, maybe a little unbelievable. The next lesson explains *why* it worked. Right now, you just need the experience of it working.

<Pitfall title="Don't Rewrite From Scratch If Something's Off">

If the fun-fact button doesn't quite work, or the colors aren't what you pictured, the instinct is to scrap it and start over with a longer, "more careful" prompt. Resist that.

The fix isn't a better first prompt — it's a second sentence. You'll learn exactly how to do that refinement in Module 2. For now, if something's off, just note it and move on to the next lesson.

</Pitfall>
