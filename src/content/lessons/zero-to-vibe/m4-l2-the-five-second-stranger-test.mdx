## 🛠️ Learning by Doing

Open your app one more time. This time, you're not adding features — you're judging it, the way a total stranger would if they landed on it with zero context.

<CodeLab title="Run the Checklist">

Go through each question honestly. Don't fix anything yet — just answer.

- **The 5-second test:** If a stranger saw this with no explanation, would they understand what it's for within 5 seconds?
- **The thumb test:** If you shrink the window or check it on your phone, does it still make sense, or does something overlap or get cut off?
- **The squint test:** Squint at the screen (or blur your eyes slightly). What stands out most? Is it the thing that actually matters most?
- **The silence test:** Is there any text or button whose purpose isn't obvious without you explaining it out loud?

Write down — even just mentally — the single worst offender. Not everything that's slightly imperfect. The *one* thing that would actually confuse or annoy a real stranger first.

</CodeLab>

<CodeLab title="Direct the Fix">

Turn your worst offender into a specific instruction, the same way you practiced in Module 2:

> "The fun-fact button just says 'Click' with no context. Change the label to something that tells the user what it does, like 'Show Me a Fact,' and make it visually stand out more than the surrounding text."

Run it. Then re-run the 5-second test on just that one element. Would a stranger get it now?

</CodeLab>

## 🔍 What You Just Practiced

You didn't need a design vocabulary to run any of those four tests. You needed to look at your own app the way you look at every other app you've ever used — with an instinct for what's confusing, honed by years of being a frustrated user yourself. That instinct is what this whole module is about trusting.

<Pitfall title="Don't Confuse 'Different' With 'Wrong'">

It's tempting to judge your app against something like a polished, professional product and feel like anything simpler must be a failure. That's the wrong comparison. The bar isn't "does this look like a Fortune 500 website" — it's "would a stranger understand what this does and how to use it." A simple, clear app passes that test easily. A cluttered, feature-packed one can fail it badly.

</Pitfall>
