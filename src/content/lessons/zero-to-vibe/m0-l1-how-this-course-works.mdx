## 🧠 A Model Called Flipped Bloom's Taxonomy

Before your first build, one short explanation — so nothing about the shape of this course feels arbitrary once you're in it.

Educators describe learning as a ladder of depth, often shown like this:

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Remember | Recall a fact or term |
| Understand | Explain an idea in your own words |
| Apply | Use it to do something new |
| Analyze | Take it apart and see how the pieces connect |
| Evaluate | Judge whether it's good, and why |
| Create | Build something original with it |

In a traditional classroom, class time gets spent at the *bottom* of this ladder — lectures covering facts, definitions, vocabulary. The *top* of the ladder — Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create, the genuinely hard cognitive work — gets pushed into homework, done alone, with no one there to help when you get stuck. You get the least support exactly when the work is hardest.

**Flipped Bloom's Taxonomy inverts that assignment.** The high-order work — doing, building, breaking, judging — happens first, with full support, right here in each lesson. The low-order work — naming terms, explaining why it worked — happens after, as the lecture, once you already have a real experience to hang those words on. Nothing hard gets left for you to face alone.

## 🔁 What This Looks Like, Every Module

Every module in this course runs the exact same shape:

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Celebrate** | A short idea that reframes what you're about to do |
| **Predict** | One quick guess — no grade — that primes you to learn |
| **Do** | You build, break, or judge something — first, before any explanation |
| **Understand** | The lecture — now that you have something real to attach it to |
| **Prove It** | Compare what you know now to what you predicted at the start |

Five modules, one shape, repeated. Once you've been through it once, you'll never have to wonder what's coming next.
