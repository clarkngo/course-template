## ✍️ What Makes a Prompt Land

Compare the vague prompts from last lesson's Pitfall box to their specific versions. The pattern underneath the good ones has three ingredients:

| Ingredient | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Specificity** | Name the exact thing, not a general feeling | "Make the button green" beats "make it pop" |
| **Context** | Say what's currently happening, not just what you want | "The counter resets to zero when clicked — it shouldn't" beats "fix the counter" |
| **One change at a time** | Bundle too many asks and it's hard to tell what worked | Three focused rounds beat one giant wishlist |

You didn't need a course to invent this pattern — you already used it instinctively in the last lesson, once you had a reason to be specific.

## 🧭 Testing Is Not Optional

The "Test" step in the loop isn't a formality — skip it and refinement has nothing to work from. Testing well just means:

- Actually run the app, don't just read about it
- Interact with it the way a real user would (click it more than once)
- Notice specifics: *not* "it feels off" but "the button text doesn't change color when I click it"

The sharper your noticing, the sharper your next refinement can be. Vague noticing produces vague refinements, which produce vague results — the same problem, one step earlier.

<Quiz question="You've just tested your app and something's wrong, but you can't quite describe it — it just 'feels off.' What should you do before refining?">
<div>

**Look again, more specifically — click through it slowly and name one concrete thing that's different from what you pictured.**

"Feels off" isn't yet a usable refinement. Slow down on the Test step until you can point at something specific: a color, a missing piece of text, a button that does nothing. That specific observation is what makes the next Refine step actually work.
